# All three period conjectures in OEIS A210456

Write $a(n)$ for the eventual period of the digital roots of the **fundamental** $n$-step Fibonacci sequence, initialized by $n-1$ zeros and a single 1. The entry gives three conjectural assertions. For every $n\ge2$, we obtain $3\mid a(n)$. For every integer $k\ge1$, with $q=3^k$, we obtain

$$
a(q-1)=3(q^2-1),\qquad a(q)=3(q^2+q+1).
$$

The second and third assertions were proposed by Fred W. Helenius in 2013. The positive-index hypothesis on $k$ matters, since $a(1)=1$. [OEIS A210456](https://oeis.org/A210456)

## Translating the period

After the initial zeros, all terms of the ordinary recurrence are positive. Digital roots then represent residues modulo 9 bijectively, with residue zero represented by 9. Let $A$ be the integer matrix advancing the state of $n$ consecutive terms. The initial state $v=e_n$ and its first $n-1$ iterates are a basis over every residue ring. Indeed, $A^jv$ has its first nonzero entry, equal to 1, in position $n-j$ for $0\le j<n$. As $A$ is invertible over the integers, eventual and initial state periods coincide modulo 9. The order of $A$ equals the period of $v$, since fixing $v$ under $A^T$ fixes every $A^jv$. Over a field the minimal and characteristic polynomials therefore agree. Both equal

$$
P_n(X)=X^n-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}X^i,\qquad (X-1)P_n(X)=X^{n+1}-2X^n+1.
$$

## Every period is a multiple of three

Realize the nonnegative integer matrix $A$ as the adjacency matrix of a finite directed graph. The integer $S_m=\operatorname{tr}(A^m)$ counts closed walks of length $m$ with a designated starting point. Consider rotation by one edge on closed walks of length $3^j$, with $j\ge2$. Walks invariant under rotation by $3^{j-1}$ edges are the triple repetitions of a closed walk of length $3^{j-1}$. All remaining walks have full rotation orbit of size $3^j$. Thus $S_{3^j}\equiv S_{3^{j-1}}\pmod9$, so $S_{3^j}\equiv S_3\pmod9$ for every $j\ge1$.

Assume $T=a(n)$ is coprime to 3. Choose $j\ge1$ with $3^j\equiv1\pmod T$. Since $A^T=I\pmod9$, we get $S_1\equiv S_{3^j}\equiv S_3\pmod9$. Newton's identities applied to $P_n$ give $S_1=1, S_2=3$, and $S_3=7$ if $n\ge3$, or $S_3=4$ if $n=2$. Both possibilities contradict the congruence. This proves the first assertion.

## The two exact periods modulo three

For a separable $P_n$ the matrix order over $\mathbb F_3$ is the exponent of the group generated by its roots in a splitting field. This follows because $P_n\mid X^m-1$ is equivalent to $A^m=I$. A finite field has cyclic multiplicative group, so its subgroup exponent equals its order. Inverting every root preserves the subgroup. Adjoining the root 1 also preserves it.

**Order $n=q-1$.** Multiplying by $X-1$ and taking reciprocals identifies the roots with the zeros of $Y^q+Y+1$. In $E=\mathbb F_{q^2}$ these form the affine line $D=\{y:\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_q}(y)=-1\}$. It has $q$ distinct nonzero points, accounting for all roots of the degree-$q$ polynomial. Distinctness also follows from its derivative 1. The element 1 belongs to $D$.

For any $b\in E\setminus\mathbb F_q$, the two linear forms $y\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(y)$ and $y\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(by)$ on $E$ are independent over $\mathbb F_q$. A relation would force $b\in\mathbb F_q$ by the nondegeneracy of the finite-field trace pairing. There is therefore a solution of both trace equations with right-hand side $-1$, giving $b=(by)/y\in D/D$. Every $c\in\mathbb F_q^*$ is also generated by $D$, since $c=(cb)/b$ for any $b\notin\mathbb F_q$. Hence the roots generate $E^*$, and the period modulo 3 is $q^2-1$.

**Order $n=q$.** The reciprocal polynomial after adjoining the factor $X-1$ has roots satisfying $Y^{q+1}+Y+1=0$. Put $E=\mathbb F_{q^3}$ and let $W=\ker\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_q}$, a two-dimensional vector space. For $z\in W\setminus\{0\}$, divide $z^{q^2}+z^q+z=0$ by $z$. Then $y=z^{q-1}$ is a root. The fibers on $W\setminus\{0\}$ are precisely the cosets of $\mathbb F_q^*$, yielding $(q^2-1)/(q-1)=q+1$ distinct roots, the full degree. The root 1 occurs since every element of $\mathbb F_q$ has trace zero in this cubic extension of characteristic 3.

The image of $u\mapsto u^{q-1}$ on $E^*$ is cyclic of order $q^2+q+1$. For each $u\in E^*$, the two planes $W$ and $u^{-1}W$ have a nonzero vector $z$ in common. Thus $z,uz\in W\setminus\{0\}$, and $u^{q-1}=(uz)^{q-1}/z^{q-1}$ is a quotient of roots. The roots generate the entire image, giving period $q^2+q+1$ modulo 3.

## Lifting to nine

If $s$ is the period of $A$ modulo 3, then $A^s=I+3B$ for an integer matrix $B$. Cubing gives $A^{3s}=I\pmod9$. Therefore $s\mid a(n)\mid 3s$. In both families above $3\nmid s$, while the preceding general argument proves $3\mid a(n)$. Thus $a(n)=3s$, as claimed.

## Independent proof of both finite-field orders using degrees

The exact periods above can also be obtained using polynomial division over $\mathbb F_3$. Let $R_n$ denote the reciprocal of $P_n$ normalized to be monic. At $n=q-1,q$ its value at 1 is nonzero, since $P_n(1)=1-n$. Adjoining the factor $Y-1$ does not change its order: $Y-1$ divides $Y^t-1$ for every positive $t$ and is coprime to $R_n$.

For $n=q-1$ the enlarged polynomial is $Y^q+Y+1$. In its quotient ring, $y^q=-y-1$, whence $y^{q^2}=y$ by Frobenius. Its constant term is a unit, so $y^{q^2-1}=1$. For each prime $p$ dividing $q^2-1$, consider $m=(q^2-1)/p=aq+b$. If $p\mid q-1$ then $a=b=(q-1)/p$. Otherwise $p\mid q+1$, in which case $a=(q+1)/p-1$ and $b=q-(q+1)/p$. In each case $0<a+b\le q-1$. The equality $y^m=1$ would give a polynomial identity $(-Y-1)^aY^b-1=0$ modulo the degree-$q$ polynomial. Its left side has degree $a+b$ and is nonzero, a contradiction. This rules out all proper divisors of $q^2-1$.

For $n=q$ the enlarged polynomial is $Y^{q+1}+Y+1$. In its quotient ring we have $y^{q+1}=-y-1$. Applying Frobenius gives $y^{q^2+q}=-y^q-1$, and multiplication by $y$ gives $y^{q^2+q+1}=1$. If $0<m<q^2$, write $m=aq+b$ with $a,b<q$. If $b\ge a$, then $y^m=1$ would yield $(-Y-1)^aY^{b-a}-1=0$ in the quotient. Its degree is $b>0$. If $b<a$, it would yield $(-Y-1)^a-Y^{a-b}=0$. Its degree is at most $a<q$ and its constant term is nonzero. Both contradictions come from the modulus having degree $q+1$. Every proper divisor of $q^2+q+1$ is below $q^2$ when $q\ge3$, proving the second order.

## Verification status

All three assertions are verified in Lean 4.19. The all-order theorem [MultinacciPeriods.digital_root_period](MultinacciPeriods.lean) includes the initial zeros, decimal digital-root recurrence and an arbitrary eventual period. Its independent algebraic proof works over $\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z$ and forces $P_n$ to divide $6(X^3-1)(X-1)^2$ if there is a period coprime to 3. This is impossible by degree for $n\ge6$ and by nonzero remainders for $n=2,3,4,5$.

For the exact formulas, [HeleniusOrders](HeleniusOrders.lean) verifies both trinomial orders over $\mathbb F_3$ by the degree argument. [HeleniusPeriods.quadratic_period and HeleniusPeriods.cubic_period](HeleniusPeriods.lean) prove that a natural number $T$ is an eventual period of the original digital-root sequence if and only if the claimed period divides $T$. This includes existence and minimality of the stated positive period for every $k\ge1$. Each final theorem uses the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The [independent checker](check_arithmetic.py) also verifies the two formulas for $1\le k\le5$, including minimality.

The trace congruence is the ordinary orbit-counting congruence for periodic walks, and the cubic trace-plane argument is the classical finite-projective-plane difference-set construction. The OEIS entry consulted still labels the three assertions as conjectures. This local note does not establish publication priority.
