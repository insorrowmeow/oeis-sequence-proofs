# Both vector algorithms for the peak-height polynomials

Let Rₙ(z) enumerate Dyck paths of semilength n by the sum, over their peaks, of height minus one. Both algorithms in [MathOverflow 477465](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/477465/elegant-algorithm-for-a140717) hold, including the author's update that directly outputs Rₙ. [OEIS A140717](https://oeis.org/A140717)

**Peak–valley identity.** For a nonempty Dyck path with r peaks, list its successive ascent and descent run lengths. Let A and D be the sets of partial sums of the respective run lengths, omitting their common endpoint n. They are (r−1)-subsets of U={1,…,n−1}. If listed increasingly they satisfy Aᵢ≥Dᵢ, precisely the condition of nonnegative valley heights. Successive differences with endpoints 0,n recover the entire path.

Replacing (A,D) by (U\D,U\A) is an involution. Validity follows by counting points in every initial interval: D has at least as many as A, hence the complement of A has at least as many as the complement of D. The sum of all valley heights is Δ=sum(A)−sum(D). The sum of all peak heights is n+Δ, by summing the cumulative up steps through each peak minus the cumulative down steps before it. The involution preserves Δ and gives n−r valleys. Consequently the peak weight exponent n+Δ−r of the original path equals the sum of (valley height + 1) in its image. The empty path has both weights one. The argument uses only the total statistic, so it requires no assumption about the distribution at individual heights.

**Reading the sweep.** Use λ=1 for the first algorithm and λ=z for the updated one. Before a pass, denote the active suffix of the vector by v₀,v₁,… . After dropping the working cell, its new entries are

    wₕ = vₕ₊₁ + λ Σ_{j=0}^h zʲ vⱼ.

Indeed, immediately before old cell h+1 is processed the working cell is Σ_{j=0}^h zʲ vⱼ. The simultaneous assignments then give the formula. Each pass shortens the needed input by one, exactly as in the finite vector.

This transfer counts nonnegative walks starting at height h. A step may rise by one with weight 1 or land at j≤h with weight λ zʲ. Initial all-ones entries permit any terminal height. Thus the i-th output, i≥1, counts length-(i−1) walks starting at zero. Append a compulsory step back to zero with weight one, including a flat step if already at zero. We get all length-i excursions with the last step exempt from the λ factor.

Replace each step from h to j by one up step followed by h+1−j down steps. This bijects such excursions with ordinary Dyck paths of semilength i: split the path before each up step to recover the walk. Every non-rising step except the last ends a descent followed by an ascent, hence gives exactly one internal valley at height j. The resulting path weight is the product of λ zʲ over its valleys.

For λ=z this is exactly the valley statistic above, so the output is Rᵢ(z). For λ=1, factor a Dyck path at all returns to zero. Valleys at the joins have weight one. In a primitive factor of semilength m+1, remove its first up and last down step. Its valleys become exactly the valleys of a Dyck path of semilength m lowered by one; their original weights zʲ now give the height-plus-one statistic. This also works for the factor UD. The primitive-factor polynomial is therefore Rₘ(z). The unique ordered factorization, including the empty path, gives the generating series 1/(1−Σₘ Rₘ(z)xᵐ⁺¹), as required.

[Lean 4.19.0](DyckValleys.lean) verifies preservation of the prefix inequalities and the weight identity for arbitrary finite run-end sets. Shifting the universe from {1,…,n−1} to zero-based indices preserves the differences of sums, since the sets have equal cardinality. The walk encoding and the vector-to-walk transfer are written proofs.
