# Equilateral triangles touching all four faces

Peter Kagey's [OEIS A334386](https://oeis.org/A334386) conjectures that the number \(a(n)\) of equilateral triangles in an \(n\)-point-per-edge tetrahedral grid touching all four faces satisfies \(a(n)\equiv4(n-1)\pmod {12}\) for \(n>0\). Vertices are unordered and the triangles have positive side length. We prove that exactly \(4(n-1)\) of the triangles have a nontrivial rotational symmetry; the others occur in orbits of size twelve.

## The integer lattice and the half-turn obstruction

Put \(N=n-1\). Represent the vertices by nonnegative integer quadruples of sum \(N\). The four coordinate unit vectors are vertices of a regular tetrahedron in the hyperplane of sum one. Thus the Euclidean metric inherited from \(\mathbb R^4\) is the tetrahedral-grid metric up to scale. For \(N>0\), face \(i\) is given by \(x_i=0\). A triangle touches it exactly when some vertex has \(x_i=0\), by nonnegativity. The rotation group \(A_4\) acts by even coordinate permutations.

Consider the half-turn \((12)(34)\). Its fixed points in the sum-\(N\) hyperplane lie on a line, so it cannot fix three noncollinear vertices individually. If it preserves a triangle, it must exchange \(A=(a,b,e,f)\) and \(B=(b,a,f,e)\) and fix \(C=(c,c,d,d)\). Let \(M\) be the midpoint of \(AB\). Put \(u=2c-a-b,\ v=a-b,\ w=e-f\). From \(a+b+e+f=2c+2d\) one obtains

\[
|CM|^2=u^2,\qquad |AM|^2=(v^2+w^2)/2.
\]

Equilaterality gives \(2u^2=3(v^2+w^2)\). First \(3\mid u\). Then \(3\mid v^2+w^2\); squares modulo three are zero or one, hence \(3\mid v,w\). Dividing all three variables by three produces a new integral solution. Infinite descent on \(|u|\) gives \(u=0\), whence \(v=w=0\) and \(A=B\). The other two half-turns are conjugate to this one. No nondegenerate lattice triangle is fixed by any half-turn.

## Counting the order-three stabilizers

A triangle stabilizer in \(A_4\) has odd order by Cauchy's theorem and divides twelve, so its order is one or three. Consider one of the four order-three subgroups, generated by \((123)\). Its pointwise fixed set in the sum-\(N\) hyperplane is a line. Each triangle fixed by the subgroup therefore consists of the orbit of a single non-fixed grid point. Conversely any such orbit is an equilateral triangle, since the three points are distinct and their mutual distances coincide.

The fourth coordinate is constant on the orbit. For the orbit to touch the fourth face it must vanish; to touch the other three faces at least one of the remaining coordinates must vanish. There are precisely \(3N\) triples of nonnegative integers with sum \(N\) and a zero coordinate, since there are three corners and \(N-1\) interior points on each of three edges. None has three equal entries for \(N>0\). The subgroup fixes precisely \(N\) triangles, one for each orbit of three such triples. The same holds for all four subgroups; their sets of fixed triangles are disjoint because each nontrivial stabilizer has order three. This gives \(4N\) triangles with nontrivial stabilizers. Orbit-stabilizer partitions the others into sets of twelve. At \(N=0\) the grid has one vertex, so the congruence also holds.

## A consequence for all triangles

The same argument counts the symmetric triangles without any face requirement. If \(b(n)\) is the total in [A334581](https://oeis.org/A334581), each order-three subgroup fixes exactly

\[
F(N)=\frac{\binom{N+3}{3}-(\lfloor N/3\rfloor+1)}{3}
\]

triangles. Indeed the binomial counts grid points, and the axis contains exactly the points \((x,x,x,N-3x)\) for \(0\le x\le\lfloor N/3\rfloor\). Thus \(b(n)\equiv4F(N)\pmod {12}\). This is a corollary, not a second solution of a separately sourced conjecture.

## Verification and provenance

[Kagey.lean](Kagey.lean) formalizes the integer descent and the exclusion of an exchanged pair in a half-turn-invariant equilateral triangle. The group action, counting and final congruences have written proofs above. The companion [enumeration](verify.py) counts triangles directly from their three squared distances and face incidences. Kagey's [grid-polytope article](https://peterkagey.com/blog/2021/04/polytopes-in-the-grid/) and [Anders Kaseorg's counting algorithm](https://codegolf.stackexchange.com/a/204453) provide computational context.

There is a discrepancy within the OEIS entry at \(n=6\). Its data give 32 while the worked example gives 28. Direct enumeration gives 32. The fourth example triangle, scaled by five, has vertices \((0,0,2,3),(0,3,2,0),(3,1,0,1)\). Its orbit under all coordinate permutations has 12 members: its stabilizer has order two, generated by swapping coordinates two and four. Indeed only the third vertex has a coordinate equal to one, so it is fixed and the first and third coordinates must remain fixed individually. The four orbit sizes in the example are therefore \(4,8,8,12\).
