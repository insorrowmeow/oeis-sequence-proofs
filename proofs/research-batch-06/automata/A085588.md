# When does the Rule 150 period equal the ring width?

Let \(a(n)\) be the eventual period of the single-seed configuration of Rule 150 on the cyclic ring of \(n\ge3\) cells. Roman Khrabrov conjectured on 17 August 2024 that every \(n\) with \(a(n)=n\) belongs to A115770. The following classification proves his implication.

**Theorem.** \(a(n)=n\) if and only if \(n=2^s(2^k-1)\), where \(s\ge0\) and \(k\ge3\). In binary these are the words consisting of one run of at least three ones followed by zero or more zeros.

## Reduction to a condition on binary digits

Write \(n=2^s m\) with \(m\) odd. Encode the configuration by its generating polynomial in \(R_n=\mathbf F_2[X]/(X^n-1)\). One time step multiplies by \(T=X^{-1}+1+X\). A single initial cell gives the orbit \(1,T,T^2,\ldots\). Thus a positive integer \(d\) is a multiple of the eventual period exactly when \(T^J(T^d+1)=0\) for some \(J\).

Suppose that \(a(n)\mid n\). At any \(m\)-th root of unity \(z\) with \(T(z)\ne0\), evaluation gives \(T(z)^n=1\). In characteristic two this implies \(T(z)^m=1\), since \(n=2^s m\). At every other \(m\)-th root, \(T\) itself vanishes. The roots of \(X^m-1\) are simple, so \(T(T^m+1)=0\) in \(R_m\).

In \(R_m\) put \(B=(1+X)^m\) and \(D=B(X^3)+B(X)\). Since \(X(1+X)T=1+X^3\) and \(X^m=1\), we have \(D=B(T^m+1)\), hence \(TD=0\). For \(m>1\) the coefficient \(B_0\) vanishes. For \(1\le j<m\), its coefficient \(B_j\) is 1 exactly when every binary one-bit of \(j\) occurs in \(m\). Indeed, \( (1+X)^m=\prod_{i:\,\mathrm{bit}_i(m)=1}(1+X^{2^i})\) before reduction. Only the coefficients at 0 and \(m\) collide.

* If \(3\nmid m\), the numerator \(X^2+X+1\) of \(T\) has no root among the \(m\)-th roots of unity. Hence \(T\) is a unit and \(D=0\). Multiplication by 3 permutes residues modulo \(m\), and \(B_{3j\bmod m}=B_j\). Let \(h\) be the index of the top bit of \(m>1\). Bit 0 is set. Whenever bit \(i\) is set and \(0\le i\le h-2\), we have \(3\cdot2^i<m\) and \(B_{3\cdot2^i}=B_{2^i}=1\). The binary digits of \(3\cdot2^i\) force bit \(i+1\) of \(m\) to be set. All bits through the top are set.
* If \(3\mid m\), the identity \(TD=0\) gives \(D_{j-1}+D_j+D_{j+1}=0\) with cyclic indices. Thus \(D_{j+3}=D_j\). The coefficients of \(B(X^3)\) outside the residue class 0 modulo 3 vanish even after reduction. Consequently \(B_j\) is constant on each of the other two classes. Both constants equal 1 since \(B_1=B_{m-1}=1\), using oddness of \(m\). Each power of two below \(m\) belongs to one of these two classes. Every binary digit of \(m\) is therefore set.

In both cases \(m=2^k-1\); \(m=1\) has this form as well.

## The exact period at the surviving widths

For \(m=2^k-1\), \(T^{2^k}=T\) in \(R_m\), by the Frobenius identity and \(2^k\equiv1\bmod m\). Raising the Laurent-polynomial congruence to the power \(2^s\) makes it valid modulo \((X^m-1)^{2^s}=X^n-1\). This gives \(T^{2^s}(T^n+1)=0\) in \(R_n\), hence \(a(n)\mid n\).

Suppose \(m\ge7\). First let \(d=a(m)\mid m\). At each \(m\)-th root except possibly the two primitive cube roots, \(T^d=1\). The monic polynomial \((1+X+X^2)^d-X^d\), of degree \(2d\), has at least \(m-2\) distinct roots. No proper divisor of the odd integer \(m\) can satisfy \(2d\ge m-2\), since \(d\le m/3\) and \(m>6\). Thus \(a(m)=m\). Reduction from \(R_n\) to \(R_m\) implies \(m\mid a(n)\).

Take a primitive \(m\)-th root \(z\) in an algebraic closure. It differs from 1 and from the primitive cube roots, so \(T(z)\ne0\) and \(T'(z)=1+z^{-2}\ne0\). Write \(a(n)=2^e u\) with \(u\) odd. The root \(z\) of \(T^u-1\) is simple, since its derivative is \(uT^{u-1}T'\). It therefore has multiplicity \(2^e\) in \(T^{a(n)}-1\). Eventual periodicity implies that \(T^J(T^{a(n)}-1)\) vanishes modulo \(X^n-1\) for some \(J\). The factor \(T^J\) is nonzero at \(z\), while \(z\) has multiplicity \(2^s\) in \(X^n-1\). It follows that \(e\ge s\). Together with \(m\mid a(n)\mid n\), this gives \(a(n)=n\).

If \(m=1\), \(T^{n/2}=1\) in \(R_n\) because the two shifted monomials coincide. If \(m=3\), \(T^2=T\) in \(R_3\), so the lifted identity shows that \(a(n)\mid2^s\). Neither case gives equality in the domain \(n\ge3\).

## Consequences for A115770

A115770 contains precisely the nonnegative integers \(v\) satisfying \(7v=v\mathbin{\rm xor}2v\mathbin{\rm xor}8v\). For \(v=2^k-1,\ k\ge3\), the first two terms of the xor give \(2^k+1\). Bit \(k\) of \(8v\) is set and bit 0 is clear, so the final xor gives \(8v-2^k+1=7v\). Shifting all operands by the same number of bits preserves equality. This proves Khrabrov's implication.

The first exception to the reverse implication in the OEIS domain is **455**, with \(a(455)=4095\). The entry gives 445, but \(7\cdot445=3115\) whereas the xor is 3887. At 455 both products equal 3185. The accompanying verifier checks all smaller admissible widths and calculates the period at 455 directly. Equivalently the binary run criterion for A115770 requires one-runs of length at least three and internal zero-runs of length at least three, so the smallest admitted number with two one-runs is \(111000111_2=455\).

## Sources and attribution

The polynomial model and period bounds have a long history. Kaneko's Theorem 3.1 gives a period bound in the invertible part of the displayed family. Bojić's Theorem 8.19 gives the order there in terms of an LCM. Section 2.3 leaves its evaluation for Mersenne odd parts open. The TrureTuring proof listed below establishes that evaluation using a polynomial degree bound. The digit argument above supplies necessity, and allowing two missing roots extends the degree argument to odd parts divisible by three. The displayed theorem proves the stated OEIS conjecture. Priority for the full classification has not been established.

Lean 4.19.0 proves the complete classification for every width from 3 onwards in [PeriodLower.lean](PeriodLower.lean), theorem `exact_width_iff`, and the original XOR implication in [Khrabrov.lean](Khrabrov.lean), theorem `khrabrov`. The cyclic group algebra over the binary field directly represents the automaton. The library lemmas [PeriodBits.lean](PeriodBits.lean), [Kernel.lean](Kernel.lean) and [PeriodNecessity.lean](PeriodNecessity.lean) derive the Mersenne shape of the odd part from an eventual repeat a width apart. [check_automata.py](check_automata.py) also checks widths 3 through 10,000 and the period at 455 by two computations.

The formal proof of minimality uses the support of the evolving configuration. For \(n=2^s m\), \(m=2^k-1\ge7\), row \(J=2^s\) repeats after \(n\) steps. Its least positive period \(d\) divides \(n\). If \(n/d\ge3\), then \(2(J+d)<n\). The site at distance \(J+d\) is one at time \(J+d\), because only the path consisting entirely of right moves can reach it. It is zero at time \(J\). If \(n/d=2\), put \(p=2^{s-1}\). All occupied sites at the two times are multiples of \(p\), so divide their spatial indices by \(p\). In the resulting ring of width \(2m\), the two rows are \(T^2\) and \(T^{m+2}=T^{2^k}T\). At site 1 the former is zero, whereas the latter is one. The shifts by \(\pm(m+1)\) in \(T^{2^k}\) contribute only near site \(m\), since \(m\ge7\). Hence every proper divisor fails.
