# A census of the bounded faces in the circle arrangement A401663

[A401663](https://oeis.org/A401663) places $n$ unit circles at the centers $u_j=(\cos j,\sin j)$, $0\le j<n$, and counts bounded faces by degree. For $n\ge5$, put a $+$ at each direction $j$ modulo $2\pi$ and a $-$ at its antipode. Read the $2n$ signs in cyclic order. Let $b_n$ be half the number of changes of sign, including the last-to-first transition. For $2\le n\le4$ set $b_n=3$.

**Theorem.** There are no bounded faces of degree other than two, three and four. Their respective numbers are
$$
b_n,\qquad 3n-2b_n,\qquad n(n+1)/2-3n+b_n .
$$
Each $b_n$ is odd, and $b_{n+1}-b_n\in\{0,2\}$. This proves the OEIS observations on the degrees, the digon counts and the two possible increments of the triangle counts. The assertion about plateau endpoints in a particular congruence class is separate.

**Inversion.** On the sphere obtained by adjoining infinity to the plane, inversion interchanges the common origin with infinity. After rescaling, the image circles are the tangent lines $u_j\cdot x=1$ to the unit disk. No parallels occur because $\pi$ is irrational, and no triple concurrency occurs in the finite plane since at most two tangents to the disk can pass through a point. The all-negative cell $C_0$, defined by $u_j\cdot x<1$ for every $j$, contains zero and corresponds to the unique unbounded original face. Each of its $n$ supporting lines gives exactly one side, since its own contact point satisfies all other inequalities strictly. Every other cell is convex. Its supporting side intervals become edges under inversion; the two rays bounding an unbounded cell end at the same vertex on the sphere. In particular, the number of sides is preserved.

**At most four sides.** For a point outside $C_0$ the nonempty set $I$ of violated inequalities consists of the normals in an open arc of length strictly less than $\pi$: these are the directions making angle less than $\arccos(1/|x|)$ with $x$. Thus $I$ is a consecutive cyclic interval. Across a side of the cell exactly one sign changes. When $I$ is a proper nonempty cyclic interval, a one-element deletion which leaves an interval (possibly empty) can only delete one of its endpoints, and a one-element addition must use one of its two immediate neighbors. Exceptions when the *result* is the full or empty set still obey the same bound. The cell has at most four sides because convexity gives at most one side for each changed sign. If $I$ is the whole set, all normals lie in a single arc shorter than $\pi$. Any arc shorter than $\pi$ containing both extreme normals in this order contains all intermediate normals, so only an extreme normal may be removed. No cell is a monogon: a one-sided cell of a line arrangement would be an entire half-plane, cut by any other nonparallel line.

**Counting wedges.** For $n\ge3$ a two-sided cell of the tangent lines is an unbounded wedge with one finite vertex. The pair $i,j$ gives such a wedge if and only if their crossing is outermost on both lines. Indeed, a two-sided wedge uses the two terminal rays. Conversely, the free ray on each line starting at a mutual outermost crossing bounds one convex sector of angle less than $\pi$; another line traversing its interior would have to cross at least one ray, since the sector contains no complete line. The wedge is unique, as each line has at least one other crossing. $C_0$ has $n$ sides and is excluded. At $n=2$ all four line cells are wedges, of which three are counted.

Along line $i$, oriented tangentially with angular coordinate increasing, the crossing with $j$ has coordinate $\tan((j-i)/2)$ relative to the tangency point, using the representative $j-i\in(-\pi,\pi)$. This follows by solving $u_j\cdot(u_i+t\,u_i^\perp)=1$. For $n=3,4$ all numerals $j$ lie in $[0,3]\subset[0,\pi)$, so these coordinates increase with $j$ after omission of $i$. At $n=3$ all three pairs are mutually extreme. At $n=4$ the three pairs are $\{0,1\},\{0,3\},\{2,3\}$.

For $n\ge5$, the largest gap between successive normals is strictly less than $\pi$, since the first five normals already have this property. Hence on each side of $i$ its outermost crossing is with one of the two normal directions immediately bracketing the antipode of $i$. A mutual extremum is equivalent to adjacency of $-u_i$ and $+u_j$ in the combined sign order. To see this, take the short arc between those directions. It has length strictly less than $\pi$. Extremality on line $i$ says the arc contains no other normal direction in its interior: the alternative long arc cannot be empty by the gap bound. Extremality on line $j$ says its antipodal arc contains no normal, equivalently the first arc contains no antinormal. Together these are exactly adjacency. Each mutually extreme unordered pair contributes two antipodal mixed adjacencies, proving the formula for $b_n$.

**Parity and increments.** Starting at any sign and reading $n$ steps around the signed circle ends at the antipodal sign, because the second half is the signwise complement of the first. The number of changes in this half-tour is odd and is precisely $b_n$. Inserting a new normal and antipode inserts complementary signs into distinct antipodal gaps. In a single gap the number of transitions increases either by zero or by two; the two increases agree. Thus $b_n$ increases by zero or by two. The small cases join since $b_4=b_5=3$, the latter by sorting $0,1,2,3,4$ and their antipodes with $3<\pi<22/7$.

**Euler count.** On the sphere the $n$ circles have $1+\binom n2$ vertices and $n^2$ edges (each circle has the common vertex and $n-1$ further vertices). Euler's formula gives $n(n+1)/2$ bounded faces. Their degree sum is $2n^2-n$, removing the $n$ sides of the exterior face. Substitution yields the stated triangle and quadrilateral counts.

**A plateau outside the observed progression.** The [rational angle-order certificate](circle_plateau.py) uses bracketing alternating-series sums in Machin's formula for $\pi$ to certify every comparison in the circular order. At $n=332,333,688,689$, it gives respectively $331,333,333,335$ digons. By monotonicity the maximal constant interval is exactly $333\le n\le688$. The last index 688 and the next index 689 are respectively 28 and 29 modulo 44. Thus the observation about endpoints of the form $44k+4$ does not extend to all long plateaus. No boundary degeneracy can affect these comparisons: all normals and antinormals are distinct by irrationality of $\pi$.

[Lean](CircleDigons.lean) checks the parity relation for any finite Boolean sign walk and the exact change under complementary gap insertions. The geometry of inversion, the correspondence of extreme pairs, and Euler's formula are applied in writing.
