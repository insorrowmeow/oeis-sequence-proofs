# Three questions on esthetic numbers

Let $T(q,k)$ count positive $k$-digit integers in base $q\ge2$ whose consecutive digits differ by one. Thus a leading zero is forbidden. We prove Chai Wah Wu's three conjectures of October 21, 2024, on this array. The diagonal identities below hold throughout the domain $k\ge1$ of the array. The recurrence statement is already a consequence of the spectral formula of De Koninck and Doyon (2009). Our short proof also specifies the minimal recurrence. [OEIS A377000](https://oeis.org/A377000) · [Original questions in the OEIS data](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oeis/oeisdata/main/seq/A377/A377000.seq) · [De Koninck and Doyon](https://www.labmath.uqam.ca/~annales/volumes/33-2/PDF/155-164.pdf)

## One bijection

Let $P_q$ be the path on $0,1,\dots,q-1$, with edges between consecutive integers. Given digits $d_1,\dots,d_k$ counted by $T(q,k)$, prepend $d_1-1$. This is a bijection onto walks of **$k$ steps** on $P_q$ whose first step goes up. Drop the first vertex for the inverse. The reflection $i\mapsto q-1-i$ pairs every upward-first walk with exactly one downward-first walk. If $W(q,k)$ is the number of all walks of $k$ steps, then $2T(q,k)=W(q,k)$.

## 1. Meaningful compositions

Let $q=2m$. In dimension $2m-1$, the standard list of first-order differential operations has one map in each direction between successive types $0,1,\dots,m-1$, and one map from type $m-1$ to itself. For dimension one this list consists of the ordinary derivative alone. A meaningful formal composition of order $k$ is a string of $k$ operations in which adjacent types match. Its count includes formally composable strings whose resulting operator vanishes. This is the definition of meaningful composition used in the question. [Malešević, odd-dimensional list and composition rule](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0409287)

Project digit $i$ to type $\min(i,2m-1-i)$. This folds $P_{2m}$ into exactly the type graph above, sending the central edge to the single loop. Given a type walk and one of its two possible starting digits, its lift is unique, step by step. In particular the loop forces the lift across the central edge. The two lifts are reflections of each other. For $k\ge1$ exactly one lift starts upward, so the bijection above identifies the compositions with the digit strings. This proves conjecture 1 for every even $q\ge2$.

## 2. Recurrence for every fixed base

Write $A$ for the adjacency matrix of $P_q$ and $\mathbf{1}$ for the all-ones column. Then $2T(q,k)=\mathbf{1}^t A^k\mathbf{1}$. Cayley–Hamilton gives an integer-coefficient linear recurrence for every $q\ge2$. This proves conjecture 2.

More precisely, the minimal characteristic polynomial for the sequence starting at $k=1$ has the **distinct nonzero** roots

$$
  2\cos\frac{j\pi}{q+1},\qquad 1\le j\le q,\quad j\text{ odd},\quad 2j\ne q+1.
$$

Indeed the eigenvector for index $j$ has coordinates $\sin(ij\pi/(q+1))$, $1\le i\le q$, by the sine addition rule. The sum of its coordinates vanishes for even $j$ and equals $\cot(j\pi/(2(q+1)))>0$ for odd $j$ by the finite sine sum. The squared projection of $\mathbf{1}$ is therefore positive for every odd $j$. A zero eigenvalue contributes zero at all positive indices. Distinct nonzero geometric sequences are linearly independent by the Vandermonde determinant. Hence every listed factor is necessary. The polynomial is integral as well: set $U_{-1}=0,U_0=1,U_{i+1}=XU_i-U_{i-1}$. For $q=2m$ the polynomial is $U_m-U_{m-1}$. For $q=2m+1$ it is $U_{m+1}-U_{m-1}$, with the factor $X$ removed when $m$ is even. These formulas follow from $U_r(2\cos t)=\sin((r+1)t)/\sin t$.

## 3. The diagonal and its neighboring diagonals

For a word $w$ of $k$ signs $+1,-1$ take the partial sums from time zero and write $H(w)$ and $L(w)$ for their maximum and minimum. A walk with these steps can start at any integer from $-L(w)$ through $q-1-H(w)$. If $q\ge k$, its number of possible starts is exactly $q-H(w)+L(w)$, including the possibility of zero starts. Negating all signs interchanges maximum with minus minimum. Put $S_k=\sum_w H(w)$. We obtain for $q\ge \max(2,k)$, $k\ge1$,

$$
 T(q,k)=q2^{k-1}-S_k. \tag{1}
$$

Appending a sign to a walk increases its old maximum by one exactly when the old endpoint is its maximum and the appended sign is $+1$. Reversing the order of the old steps identifies walks ending at their maximum with walks all of whose partial sums are nonnegative. For length $j$ there are $M_j=\binom{j}{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}$ such walks. To see this, for $i\le j/2$ down-steps the count is $\binom ji-\binom j{i-1}$: reflect the prefix through the first visit to $-1$ for the excluded walks. Summing this difference telescopes. Thus $S_0=0$ and

$$
 S_{k+1}=2S_k+M_k.
$$

With $s=\sqrt{1-4x^2}$ having constant term 1, the central binomial series and $\binom{2r+1}{r}=\binom{2r+2}{r+1}/2$ give

$$
 M(x)=\sum_{j\ge0}M_jx^j=\frac1s+\frac{1/s-1}{2x},
 \qquad S(x)=\frac{xM(x)}{1-2x}.
$$

Let $B_0=1$ and $B_k=T(k+2,k)$ for $k\ge1$. Equation (1) gives $B_k=(k+2)2^{k-1}-S_k$, also valid at the auxiliary index zero. Hence

$$
 \sum_{k\ge0} B_kx^k
 =\frac{1-x}{(1-2x)^2}-\frac{xM(x)}{1-2x}
 =\frac{3-4x-\sqrt{1-4x^2}}{2(1-2x)^2}.
$$

This is the defining generating function of A182555 and proves conjecture 3. The first actual entries $T(3,1),T(4,2),T(5,3),T(6,4),T(7,5)$ are $2,5,12,29,68$. [OEIS A182555](https://oeis.org/A182555)

In fact $T(k+c,k)=B_k+(c-2)2^{k-1}$ for every $c\ge0, k\ge1, k+c\ge2$. In particular $T(k,k)=B_k-2^k$ for $k\ge2$. Subtracting $1/(1-2x)$ from the series of $B$ gives $(1-\sqrt{1-4x^2})/(2(1-2x)^2)$, the recorded generating function of A206603. This confirms the separate tentative cross-reference in the A377000 entry. For example $T(4,4)=13$. [OEIS A206603, formula](https://oeis.org/A206603/internal)

## Verification and scope

Run `python verify_esthetic.py` in this directory. It compares exact digit counts against operator compatibility from the published operation labels, all seven sample recurrences from A377000, coefficients of the square-root series, and maxima of weighted addition triangles.

The fixed-base recurrence follows from the 2009 formula. The walk count also belongs to an older literature on path homomorphisms. In our vertex-count convention $W(q,k)=|\mathrm{Hom}(P_{k+1},P_q)|$. Eggleton and Morayne's Theorem 3.4 gives the middle diagonal $W(k+1,k)=(k+1)2^k-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}2^{k-j}\binom{j}{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}$. This is exactly twice (1) at $q=k+1$ after iterating the recurrence for $S_k$. The neighboring diagonals follow by changing the number of starting digits. The OEIS source still labels the three questions as conjectures. The explicit links to A182555, A206603 and the differential operations have the proofs above. Historical priority for those identifications is unestablished. [Eggleton and Morayne, *A Note on Counting Homomorphisms of Paths*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-012-1261-0)
