# The square sum of A347205, refined by number of one-bits

The conjecture of [MathOverflow 486776](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/486776/sequence-that-sums-up-to-a196148) holds at every index. In fact, for 0≤p≤n one has

    Σ b(k)² = (2n+1)! (n+1)! / ((2p+1)! (2n−2p+1)! (p+1)! (n−p+1)!),

where the sum is over 0≤k<2ⁿ with p one-bits. The summand on the right is an existing enumeration of triples of lattice paths. The link to the question is the identification of the recursively defined b with the number of paths dominated by one binary word. [Wood, Blythe and Evans, section 6.3.1, especially equation (110) and the statement immediately following it](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/134846892/Wood_et_al_2020_J._Phys._A_3A_Math._Theor._10.1088_1751_8121_ab73aa.pdf)

### From the recurrence to paths

Write the one-bit positions of k in increasing order s₁,…,sₚ, starting with position zero at the right. Let D(k) count increasing lists of nonnegative positions t₁,…,tₚ with tᵢ≤sᵢ for every i. There is one empty choice. If k=2ᵐ(2h+1), its lowest bit is at m and its remaining bits are those of h shifted by m+1. Choose t₁=i between 0 and m. Remove that first position and shift the remaining selected and bounding positions down by i+1. The resulting upper bounds are exactly the bit positions of 2^(m−i)h. Adding i+1 back and prepending i reverses the construction. Consequently

    D(2ᵐ(2h+1)) = Σ_{j=0}^m D(2ʲh).

This is the recurrence defining b in the question. Each index on the right is smaller than the one on the left, so D=b. The argument also applies to powers of two and k=0 via the empty choice.

Read the n bit positions in increasing order as n time steps. A chosen subset of p positions encodes a path of north steps in those positions and east steps otherwise, ending at (n−p,p). Coordinatewise earlier north steps are exactly the condition that the path has at least the prescribed height at each time. Thus b(k)² counts ordered pairs of paths lying weakly above the path of k, with the same endpoint. There is no ordering condition between the two chosen paths. Reading all three step words backward exchanges above and below because their final heights agree. This gives triples with one path weakly above both others, as in the cited enumeration. If the convention for particle steps is reversed, p and n−p exchange places and the stated factorial ratio is symmetric.

For an equivalent check on the model, record the differences of the two chosen heights from the bounding height. These start and finish at (0,0), stay nonnegative, and have allowed increments E,W,N,S,NE,SW together with two distinguishable zero moves. The two zero moves represent the triples 000 and 111. Treating them as one would already give the wrong answer at n=1.

### Coefficients and verification

Summing the factorial ratio over p is precisely (n+1)!(2n+1)! times the coefficient of xⁿ in the square of Σ xʲ/((j+1)!(2j+1)!). This proves the original identity, including n=0. At n=4 the five contributions by bit count are 1,30,84,30,1, totaling 146.

[DominatedBits.lean](DominatedBits.lean) proves the dyadic recurrence for the position count and identifies it with any b satisfying the exact recurrence of the question, for arbitrary encoded binary gaps and arbitrary shift. Every positive integer has the gap encoding obtained from its unique binary expansion. The path correspondence and application of the published enumeration are written arguments. The external enumeration has not been formalized here. [Independent fixed-weight arithmetic check](check_A196148.py)
