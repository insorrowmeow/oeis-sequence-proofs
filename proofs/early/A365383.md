# Exact eventual periods and starting columns for A365383

Let $n\ge1$. For each partition $\lambda$ of $n$, let $S_\lambda$ consist of all nonnegative sums of parts with arbitrary repetition, including zero. Write $T(n,k)$ for the number of partitions with $k\in S_\lambda$. Gus Wiseman conjectured in 2023 that the extension of row $n$ is eventually periodic with period $n$. We determine its least period and its first periodic column. [OEIS A365383](https://oeis.org/A365383)

**Theorem.** The least eventual period of row $n$ is exactly $n$. Let $K(n)$ be the least nonnegative integer such that $T(n,k+n)=T(n,k)$ for every $k\ge K(n)$. Its exact value is

| Row | First periodic column |
| --- | --- |
| $n=1,2$ | $0$ |
| odd $n=2m+1\ge3$ | $m(m-1)$ |
| $n=2m$ with even $m\ge2$ | $m(m-2)$ |
| $n=2m$ with odd $m\ge3$ | $(m-3)(m+1)$ |

## Eventual values

Let $d$ be the gcd of all parts of $\lambda$. Then $d\mid n$. Divide the parts by $d$. Their minimum $a$ and maximum $b$ are positive, and together all normalized parts have gcd one. In the directed graph of residues modulo $a$, with an edge for adding any normalized part, every residue is reachable from zero. Indeed, in a finite cyclic group the submonoid generated by these residues is a subgroup, and gcd one makes it the entire group. A shortest such path has at most $a-1$ edges. Consequently every residue has a representable member at most $(a-1)b$. Any integer greater than $(a-1)b-a$ is representable: if it were smaller than that representative in its residue, it would have to be at most the representative minus $a$. Adding copies of $a$ realizes all greater members of that residue.

Thus the normalized conductor (least $C$ for which all integers $\ge C$ are representable) is at most $(a-1)(b-1)$. For $a=1$ it is zero. The original partition eventually represents exactly the multiples of $d$.

Let $P_\ast(s)$ count partitions of $s$ whose parts have gcd one. Scaling induces a bijection between partitions of $n$ having gcd $d$ and those counted by $P_\ast(n/d)$. It follows by finiteness that, for all sufficiently large $k$,

$$
T(n,k)=\sum_{d\mid\gcd(n,k)}P_\ast(n/d).
$$

This depends only on $k\bmod n$. On multiples of $n$ every partition contributes, even before stabilization, by adding the parts of $\lambda$ once for each copy of $n$. The single-part partition $(n)$ fails to contribute on every nonmultiple. Thus any eventual period must take a sufficiently large multiple of $n$ to another multiple of $n$; it is divisible by $n$.

## Sharp beginning of periodicity

For $n\ge7$, set $L$ equal to the table entry. First consider a partition into two coprime parts $p,q$, sorted increasingly. Their conductor is $(p-1)(q-1)$. For completeness, the residue representatives $0,q,\ldots,(p-1)q$ are minimal within their residues modulo $p$, since any expression uses a nonnegative number of copies of $q$. For $p\ge2$ this makes $(p-1)q-p$ the largest missing integer. For $p=1$ every integer is represented.

Among coprime pairs with sum $n$, the product $(p-1)(q-1)$ is largest at the pair nearest the midpoint. For odd $n=2m+1$ this is $(m,m+1)$. For $n=2m$ and even $m$ use $(m-1,m+1)$, whose gcd divides two and is odd. For odd $m$ the previous candidate has even parts; $(m-2,m+2)$ works since its gcd divides four and is odd. These conductors give exactly $L$.

For a gcd-one partition into at least three parts, the remaining parts after selecting a smallest and a largest one sum to at least $a$. Hence $b\le n-2a$ and

$$
(a-1)(b-1)\le(a-1)(n-3-2(a-1))\le (n-3)^2/8 .
$$

For a partition with gcd $d\ge2$ and at least two parts, the normalized total is $s=n/d$. We have $a+b\le s$ and the original partition represents every multiple of $d$ at least $d(a-1)(b-1)$. This bound is at most $d(s-2)^2/4=(n-2d)^2/(4d)\le(n-4)^2/8$, using $2\le d\le n/2$. A single part represents all multiples of its gcd immediately.

For $n=7,8,9$ direct substitution gives $(n-3)^2/8<L$. For every $n\ge10$ one has $L\ge n^2/4-n-3\ge(n-3)^2/8$; the last inequality is $n^2-2n-33\ge0$. Hence all partitions agree with their eventual divisibility test from $k=L$ on. The extremizing coprime pair misses $L-1$ and represents $L-1+n$. Every individual membership indicator is nondecreasing along $k,k+n,k+2n,\ldots$, since $n$ is itself representable. Consequently $T(n,L-1+n)>T(n,L-1)$, proving minimality. For $n\le6$, every partition either has its smallest part equal to its gcd, or is $(2,3)$ at $n=5$. The first class represents every multiple of its gcd starting at zero; $(2,3)$ has conductor two. This gives the tabulated boundary cases.

For example, row 8 first becomes periodic at column $8$ because $7$ is the largest gap for the partition $(3,5)$. The verification program enumerates partitions and solves unrestricted coin change directly for rows $1$ through $28$. Its upper column bound follows from the proved conductor bound. The source continues to describe eventual periodicity as a conjecture. The argument uses standard numerical-semigroup facts, proved above; a search by the sequence identifier did not establish who first obtained the sharp starting columns.

Lean 4.19.0 verifies eventual periodicity and minimality in `PartitionPeriods.lean`. The file proves that the enumeration includes every partition exactly once. It obtains an eventual period directly from monotonicity. For each residue modulo $n$, the row entries form a bounded nondecreasing sequence of integers, which attains its largest value and stabilizes. The singleton partition then forces every eventual period to be a multiple of $n$. The exact starting columns follow from the separate conductor argument above.
