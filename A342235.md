# Distances in the Tetrastix graph

The vertices are integer triples whose coordinates do not all have the same parity. Edges join pairs at Manhattan distance one. OEIS A342235 asks for the second differences of the number $a(r)$ of vertices at graph distance $r$ from $(1,0,0)$. [OEIS A342235](https://oeis.org/A342235) · [David Eppstein's description](https://11011110.github.io/blog/2021/02/28/linkage.html)

**Theorem.** We have $a(0)=1$, $a(2)=12$, $a(r)=3r^2+1$ for odd $r$, and $a(r)=3r^2+2$ for even $r\ge4$. The second differences begin $5,8,6$ and then alternate $4,8$, as conjectured.

## Exact distance

Write $a,b,c$ for the absolute displacements of a target vertex from $(1,0,0)$ in the three coordinate directions. Its graph distance is $a+b+c$, except when $a>0$ and $b=c=0$, when it is $a+2$. The latter case requires even $a$ because the target is a vertex.

At any vertex one coordinate has a different parity from the other two. Call its index the state, one of $X,Y,Z$. A step in the state coordinate is forbidden. A step in either of the two other coordinates switches the state to the remaining coordinate. In particular an $x$-step traverses the edge $YZ$ of a triangle of states, a $y$-step traverses $XZ$, and a $z$-step traverses $XY$. Both signs of an allowed step have the same state transition.

To make a Manhattan-shortest path, use each of these steps with the sign of the respective target displacement. It remains to order the $a,b,c$ steps into a trail starting from state $X$. Take a multigraph with $a$ copies of $YZ$, $b$ of $XZ$, and $c$ of $XY$. Its degrees in order $X,Y,Z$ are $b+c,a+c,a+b$. Each is either even or odd and their sum is even. The only forbidden pattern for an Euler trail starting at $X$ is even, odd, odd. That pattern would give $b\equiv c\not\equiv a\pmod2$, making the target coordinates $(1\pm a,\pm b,\pm c)$ all of the same parity. It is excluded by the definition of a vertex. If $b+c>0$, the nonisolated edges also form a connected component containing $X$, since any two edges of the triangle meet. Euler's trail criterion now supplies the required ordering. The zero-displacement case uses the empty path.

For $a>0$, $b=c=0$, the first step cannot be in the $x$ direction. A walk must both leave the $x$ axis and return, so it costs at least $a+2$. Take one step in $y$, then all $a$ steps in $x$, then one step back in $y$. Because $a$ is even, the state just before the final step is the same as just after the first step. This realizes the bound.

## Counting spheres

Use $t$ to mark absolute displacement. One signed coordinate contributes $E=(1+t^2)/(1-t^2)$ for even displacement and $O=2t/(1-t^2)$ for odd displacement, where zero has only one sign. Among all lattice points the forbidden displacement-parity patterns are odd-even-even and even-odd-odd, respectively. The generating function counting allowed targets by Manhattan distance is therefore

$$
 (E+O)^3-OE^2-EO^2=(E+O)(E^2+EO+O^2).
$$

For each positive even $a$ there are two exceptional axial targets. Moving their weight from $t^a$ to $t^{a+2}$ changes the whole series by $2(t^2-1)t^2/(1-t^2)=-2t^2$. On substituting $E,O$ we obtain

$$
\sum_{r\ge0}a(r)t^r
=3\frac{t(1+t)}{(1-t)^3}+\frac{1}{1-t}+\frac{1}{1-t^2}-1-2t^2.
$$

The first summand contributes $3r^2$. Reading off the other terms proves the theorem.

## Sources and verification

The same graph occurs as the crystallographic **nbo** net. In a cubic cell of side two the three edge-center orbits under translations by two have one odd coordinate, and the three face-center types have two odd coordinates. Nearest neighbors are exactly the allowed unit steps above. The first ten shell sizes had already appeared in the crystallographic literature by 2005. The proof here gives all distances and all shell sizes. Historical priority for the all-radius formula is unestablished. [MOFplus net data](https://www.mofplus.org/nets/net/nbo) · [2005 publication, note 12](https://www.chem.ucla.edu/dept/Faculty/yaghi/pubs/2004-2005/TransMtlOrgFrm-InorgChem2005.pdf)

Lean 4.19.0 checks the vertexwise distance formula for every valid integer triple in `Tetrastix.lean`. The theorem `Tetrastix.exact_distance` constructs a walk of the claimed length and bounds the length of every walk from below. The generating-function calculation above gives the shell sizes. The accompanying Python program compares direct breadth-first search with both formulas through radius 40.
