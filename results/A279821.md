# A counterexample to Sun’s eleven-term conjecture for central trinomial coefficients

Let `T(n)` be the coefficient of `x^n` in `(1 + x + x²)^n`. In 2016, Zhi-Wei Sun listed eleven composite integers `m` that are not squares of primes and satisfy `T(m) ≡ 1 (mod m)`. He conjectured that the list was complete. The integer **1,316,632,279** is an additional member. Its index in the ordered sequence is undetermined here. [Sun’s OEIS entry A279821](https://oeis.org/A279821/internal)

## Proof

Write `m = 31q`, where `q = 42,472,009`. Both factors are prime. Also, `m ≡ 3 (mod 4)`, so it cannot be a square.

For a prime `p` and `0 ≤ b < p`, the central coefficients satisfy `T(ph+b) ≡ T(h) T(b) (mod p)`. To see this, express `T(n)` as the constant term of `(x⁻¹+1+x)^n`. In characteristic `p`, its `p`th power for exponent one is `x⁻ᵖ+1+xᵖ`. In the product `(x⁻ᵖ+1+xᵖ)^h (x⁻¹+1+x)^b`, exponents in the first factor are multiples of `p`, and those in the second have absolute value at most `b<p`. Only the product of the two constant terms can contribute.

* Modulo `q`, the digit rule gives `T(m) ≡ T(31)`, and `T(31) = 53,876,592,856,681 = 1 + 1,268,520q`.
* Modulo 31, the same rule gives `T(m) ≡ T(q)`. The base-31 digits of `q`, least significant first, are `25,19,20,30,14,1`. The corresponding central coefficients reduce to `4,25,1,1,9,1`. Their product is `900 ≡ 1 (mod 31)`.

The Chinese remainder theorem gives `T(m) ≡ 1 (mod m)`. The eleven listed values are all smaller than `m`. Sun’s separate conjecture concerning congruence modulo `m²` remains outside the scope of this result.


## Reproduce

From the root of this collection, run `lake env lean Sun.lean` to check
`SunTri.a279821_counterexample`. It proves compositeness, the nonsquare condition,
the coefficient congruence and exclusion from Sun’s eleven entries. The formalization
uses Lean 4.19.0. [Lean source](../Sun.lean)

Run `python results/find_witness.py` to reproduce the search. It factors
`T(p) − 1` for primes up to 31 and tests the candidate larger prime factors.
