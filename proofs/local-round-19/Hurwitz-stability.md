# The distinct-label refinement of monotone Hurwitz numbers can vanish in the upper half-plane

Xavier Coulter, Norman Do and Ellena Moskovsky ask at the end of §3 of *Topological Narayana polynomials and interlacing conjectures* whether their specific multivariate refinement is stable. In that refinement the larger endpoint of each transposition contributes a variable the first time it occurs. A polynomial is stable if its value is nonzero whenever every variable has positive imaginary part. [Original question, p. 4](https://www.matrix-inst.org.au/wp_Matrix2016/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Coulter.pdf#page=4) · [Published chapter](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-47417-0_39)

**Theorem.** For genus zero and cycle lengths $(3,3)$ the distinct-label refinement has a zero with all five arguments strictly in the upper half-plane.

## Counting the coefficients

In the notation of Definition 1 the tuple length is $6+2\cdot0-2+2=6$. Write each transposition as $(a_i,b_i)$ with $a_i<b_i$, on the letters $1,\ldots,6$. We require $b_1\le\cdots\le b_6$, a product with two cycles of length three, and a transitive subgroup generated by the transpositions. The transposition graph must therefore contain vertex 6 on an edge, so every contributing monomial contains $t_6$. A sequence with those two cycles has two possible assignments of the cycle labels. With the normalization in Definition 1, the polynomial is

$$
P(t_2,t_3,t_4,t_5,t_6)=\frac{t_6}{360}\,Q(t_2,t_3,t_4,t_5),\qquad
360=\frac{6!}{2!}.
$$

The coefficient in the table below counts tuples before assigning the cycle labels. In the column headings the first two variables are $x=t_2,\ y=t_3$. The row labels use $z=t_4,\ w=t_5$. Multiply the row and column labels to get the monomial.

| | $1$ | $x$ | $y$ | $xy$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $1$ | 360 | 120 | 312 | 72 |
| $z$ | 696 | 200 | 520 | 120 |
| $w$ | 1752 | 520 | 1352 | 312 |
| $zw$ | 2856 | 696 | 1752 | 360 |

Here is a reproducible finite derivation of the table. Start with the identity permutation, six singleton components, an empty label set, and smallest allowed upper endpoint $2$. At each of six steps, choose $b$ from the allowed lower bound through 6, and $a$ from 1 through $b-1$. Swap entries $a,b$ in the current permutation, merge the components of $a,b$, add $b$ to the label set and take $b$ as the new lower bound. Induction on the number of steps identifies these paths bijectively with the monotone transposition tuples. At the end, keep paths with one component and with every orbit of the product of size three. Group by the presence of labels 2, 3, 4 and 5. The construction generates 246,730 tuples, of which 12,000 pass both final tests. The sum of the table entries is 12,000. [Direct enumeration](verify_hive.py) · [Lean enumeration](HiveCount.lean)

## An exact zero

At $x=-5+2i$, $y=-2+i/8$ and $w=i/8$, the table gives

$$
64Q(x,y,z,w)=(-8496-7536i)+(-10704-10704i)z.
$$

It vanishes at $z=(-167+10i)/223$. Set $t_6=i$. The five imaginary parts, in order, are $2,1/8,10/223,1/8,1$, all positive. This proves the theorem directly, including the strict inequalities in the definition of stability.

The obstruction is also visible with the two real specializations $y=-2$ and $w=0$, where the table reduces to $Q(x,-2,z,0)=-8(33+3x+43z+5xz)$. Setting $x=z-8$ gives $-8(5z^2+6z+9)$, with discriminant $-144$. The exact five arguments above put the witness entirely inside the domain asked about by the authors.

## Scope and verification

The result answers the specific unnumbered stability question about the product over distinct labels in §3. The authors' Conjectures 1 and 2 in the chapter concern the diagonal specialization and its interlacing as the cycle lengths vary. For this example the diagonal specializes to $t(3+24t+46t^2+24t^3+3t^4)/3$. Multiplying by $3\cdot3$ recovers exactly the $(3,3)$ row of the authors' Appendix A, an independent check on the coefficient sums and normalization. The multivariate counterexample leaves the univariate conjectures open. [Authors' conjectures](https://www.matrix-inst.org.au/wp_Matrix2016/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Coulter.pdf#page=4) · [Appendix A, p. 26](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.04015#page=26)

There are two Lean counting implementations. The first uses explicit permutations and a partition into graph components. It checks the 16 counts by native evaluation. The second keeps cycle lengths in each graph component and marks the cycle containing the current upper endpoint. Exactly five of the six edges of a connected tuple must merge components. The remaining edge splits a cycle. When the marked cycle has length $h$, there is exactly one endpoint for each resulting marked length $1,\ldots,h-1$. After the split, cycles can only grow, so both resulting lengths must be at most three. To join a cycle of length $j$ in another component there are $j$ possible endpoints. These transitions give an independent computation of the table in the Lean kernel. [Explicit permutations](HiveCount.lean) · [Cycle-length computation](HiveCycles.lean)

The exact complex arithmetic and inequalities are in [HurwitzZero.lean](HurwitzZero.lean). [Hurwitz33.lean](Hurwitz33.lean) applies the zero certificate to each enumeration. Its theorem `kernel_not_stable` uses the cycle recurrence and the standard axioms of mathlib. The direct enumeration, cycle recurrence, and complex certificate were checked with Lean 4.19.0. From the repository root, run `lake env python proofs/local-round-19/check_lean.py`. [Rebuild script](check_lean.py)

A search by title, authors and the distinct-label statistic found no earlier explicit answer to this finite-genus stability question. A November 2026 manuscript discusses asymptotic independence of the labels as genus grows. Those limiting statements are compatible with the genus-zero example here. Priority for this counterexample has not been established. [Related manuscript, Proposition 7.2](https://github.com/ipitchford/deformed-monotone-hurwitz-root-motion/blob/main/manuscript.tex)
