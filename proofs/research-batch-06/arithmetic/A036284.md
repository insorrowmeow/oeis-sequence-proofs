# Divisibility of the binary Fibonacci masks

Let $F_0=0,F_1=1,F_{k+2}=F_{k+1}+F_k$. Form $P_n(X)$ by reading bit $n$ of $F_0,\ldots,F_{3\cdot2^n-1}$ as successive coefficients of a polynomial over $\mathbb F_2$, starting with the constant term. Let $d=2^{n-1}-1$. For every $n\ge1$ we prove

$$
(1+X^3)^d(1+X)\mid P_n(X).
$$

This gives both of Antti Karttunen's divisibility conjectures. The first quotient is recorded as A136380. The extra linear factor is a lower bound on its multiplicity, as the example $n=2$ already has more. [OEIS A036284](https://oeis.org/A036284) · [OEIS A136380](https://oeis.org/A136380) · [OEIS A136384](https://oeis.org/A136384)

For $n\ge2$ set $L=3\cdot2^{n-1}$. With $Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ one has $Q^6=5I+8Q$. Repeated squaring gives $Q^L\equiv(1+2^n)I\bmod 2^{n+1}$. Indeed, squaring $(1+2^n)I+2^{n+1}B$ gives $(1+2^{n+1})I$ modulo $2^{n+2}$, since $n\ge2$. It follows that bit $n$ of $F_{k+L}$ differs from bit $n$ of $F_k$ by $F_k\bmod2$ in $\mathbb F_2$.

Let $A$ be the mask of the first $L$ bits in column $n$. Fibonacci parity repeats with block $(0,1,1)$, so the mask of the differences between the halves is

$$
B=(X+X^2)\sum_{j=0}^{2^{n-1}-1}X^{3j}=(X+X^2)(1+X^3)^d.
$$

The last equality follows from $(1+X^3)^{2^{n-1}}=1+X^L$ and the finite geometric sum. Consequently

$$
P_n=(1+X^L)A+X^L B
=(1+X^3)^d\big[(1+X^3)A+X^L(X+X^2)\big].
$$

Each summand in the brackets is divisible by $1+X$. For $n=1$ the actual polynomial is $X^3+X^4$, giving the remaining case. This all-index assertion is checked in Lean 4.19 by theorem [FibonacciBitMasks.conjectures](FibonacciBitMasks.lean). Its axioms are the standard propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound.

The exponent $d$ is also the **exact multiplicity of $X^2+X+1$** in $P_n$. At a primitive cube root $\omega$ in $\mathbb F_4$, the bracket above has value $\omega^L(\omega+\omega^2)=1$. Since $1+X^3=(1+X)(1+X+X^2)$ has distinct roots, the claimed multiplicity follows. For $n=1$ the same conclusion follows directly from $P_1=X^3(1+X)$.

## Exact multiplicity at 1

The linear factor has multiplicity $1,3,7$ for $n=1,2,3$ and exactly $2^{n-1}$ for every $n\ge4$. Here is a separate written proof of the last assertion.

Let $b_j(u)=\lfloor u/2^j\rfloor\bmod 2$, put $e(k)=b_0(F_k)$, and, for a binary weight $w(k)$ of period dividing 6, define

$$
S_j(w)=\sum_{k=0}^{3\cdot2^{j-1}-1}w(k)b_j(F_k)\pmod2.
$$

Fix $j\ge4$ and $M=3\cdot2^{j-2}$. Since $Q^2=Q+I$, the exact matrix power $Q^M$ is an integer combination of $I,Q$. The preceding shift congruence at index $j-1$ gives integers $u,v$ with

$$
F_{k+M}=F_k+2^{j-1}W_k,\qquad W_k=(1+2u)F_k+2vF_{k+1}.
$$

The independence of $I,Q$ modulo every power of two follows by reading the off-diagonal and lower-right entries. Equivalently the coefficient statement follows directly from the doubling induction in the Lean file.

When adding $2^{j-1}W_k$ to $F_k$, bit $j$ changes by $b_1(W_k)+e(k)b_{j-1}(F_k)$ modulo 2. Here $e(k)=b_0(W_k)$ accounts for the carry from bit $j-1$. The term $w(k)b_1(W_k)$ has period dividing 6, since $(F_k,F_{k+1})\bmod4$ repeats after six steps. There are an even number of six-step blocks in $M$. Pairing terms indexed by $k,k+M$ therefore gives

$$
S_j(w)=S_{j-1}(w e)\qquad(j\ge4).
$$

Since $e^2=e$, this implies $S_n(1)=S_3(e)=0$ for all $n\ge4$. In the first twelve Fibonacci numbers only $F_7=13$ and $F_{11}=89$ are odd and have bit 3 equal to one. Hence $A(1)=0$. The derivative of the bracket in the factorization of $P_n$, evaluated at 1, is $A(1)+1=1$. Its root at 1 is simple. For $n=1,2,3$, direct binary polynomial division using the supplied checker gives the stated small multiplicities.

As a consequence, for $n\ge4$ the binary periodic column has linear complexity $3\cdot2^{n-1}+2$. Its generating function is $P_n/(1+X^{3\cdot2^n})$. The denominator is $(1+X^3)^{2^n}$, and cancellation removes $2^{n-1}+2(2^{n-1}-1)$ degrees. The denominator is a period since another half-period shift squared fixes the state modulo $2^{n+1}$.
